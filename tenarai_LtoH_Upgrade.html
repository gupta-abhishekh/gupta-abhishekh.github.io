Smartphone Upgrade Propensity – Low-End to High-End Device Targeting

Tenarai  |  Sr. Data Scientist  |  09/2025 – 11/2025

Project Overview

Built a customer value management (CVM) targeting engine for a large African mobile operator. It finds prepaid subscribers who use low-end smartphones but show clear signs that they are ready to upgrade to a higher-end device. The engine combines device specifications, data usage, spending and location behavior to create a ranked list of upgrade candidates for device offers, financing plans and personalized campaigns.

FlagConditionMeaning
0Fewer than 2 of 4 readiness scenarios metNot a priority for upgrade offers
12 or more of 4 readiness scenarios metStrong upgrade candidate, target for campaigns

The engine processed 497,075 active prepaid subscribers from a 24-week base and flagged 147,305 (29.6%) as upgrade candidates.

Key Features

  • Four readiness scenarios: Device limitation, data hunger, affordability and location presence. Each gives a separate binary flag.
  • Data-driven thresholds: Uses percentile benchmarks (P50, P75, P80) from the subscriber base, not fixed values, so the rules adjust as the base changes.
  • Device capability check: Uses screen size, resolution, manufacturing year, RAM and internal storage to find handsets that limit modern app use.
  • Affordability gate: Needs high total revenue, high data revenue and a high average daily balance together, so offers go only to subscribers who can pay.
  • Explainable output: Every candidate has 4 visible flags, so campaign teams know why each subscriber qualified.
  • Clean base: Only valid, active prepaid subscribers with more than 90 days of tenure are scored.

Problem Statement

Smartphone adoption on the network was already high. But many subscribers still used low-end handsets with small screens, low RAM and little storage. These devices limited their use of video, social media and mobile money, which held back data revenue. The operator had no reliable way to find which low-end users were ready and able to upgrade. Broad device campaigns wasted budget on subscribers who could not afford a new phone or did not need one.

Solution Approach

  1. Base creation: Built an active subscriber base from 24 weeks of data. Removed postpaid and enterprise lines, invalid MSISDN formats, records with no last-activity date and subscribers with 90 days or less of tenure.
  2. Device limitation scenario: Flagged a handset as limited if it met any one condition: screen size or resolution below the median, made in 2016 or earlier, less than 2 GB RAM or less than 32 GB storage.
  3. Data hunger scenario: Flagged subscribers above the 75th percentile on any one of these over the last 4 weeks: total data use, 4G data use, data sessions or active data days.
  4. Affordability scenario: Flagged subscribers only if all three measures were above the 75th percentile over the last 4 weeks: total revenue, data revenue and average daily balance.
  5. Location presence scenario: Measured how often a subscriber used the same main cell site over 24 weeks. A repeat count below the 80th percentile marked a mobile subscriber, who is more open to a device change.
  6. Final decision rule: Classified a subscriber as an upgrade candidate when at least 2 of the 4 scenarios were true.
  7. Validation & profiling: Checked flag rates, overlaps and candidate profiles to confirm the output was sensible before handover.
  8. Delivery & documentation: Wrote the scored output to an analytics table with a full data dictionary for the CVM and campaign teams.

Business Value

  • Targeted device sales: Device and financing offers go to subscribers with a real need and the means to pay.
  • Data revenue growth: Better devices unlock video, social media and mobile money use, which lifts data consumption.
  • Better customer experience: Heavy data users get devices that match their usage.
  • Lower campaign waste: A list of about 30% of the base replaces broad, untargeted campaigns.
  • Easy to explain: Clear flags let business teams trust the list and design offers by segment.

Challenges

  • No labelled upgrade history: There was no past data on who upgraded after an offer, so a supervised model was not possible. Built an explainable heuristic approach instead.
  • Choosing thresholds: Fixed cut-offs would go out of date. Used percentile benchmarks from the current base so thresholds adjust over time.
  • Device data gaps: Some handsets had missing or inconsistent specs in the device catalog. Used several capability checks so one missing field did not block the flag.
  • Balancing the scenarios: The location flag covers 70% of subscribers, while affordability covers under 8%. The 2-of-4 rule and a strict affordability gate kept the final list focused.
  • Data quality: Fixed MSISDN format differences and removed inactive or new subscribers to keep the base reliable.

Positioning: Where the Solution Fits in the Business

Business Context

For a mobile operator, device capability limits how much data a subscriber can use. Moving the right subscribers to better smartphones grows data revenue and customer value. The upgrade engine sits inside the operator's CVM analytics layer. It turns raw device, usage and revenue data into a target list for device and data campaigns.

Who Uses the Output

  • CVM and campaign teams: Build targeted device upgrade campaigns.
  • Device and retail teams: Plan handset stock, bundles and financing offers.
  • Data product teams: Pair new devices with data bundles to grow usage.
  • Leadership: Track device mix and data revenue growth.
Guardrails: The flag shows upgrade readiness, not a confirmed purchase intent. Campaign teams apply business rules and contact rules before any offer goes out.
